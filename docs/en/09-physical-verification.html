09 Physical Verification (DRC / LVS)

Does it follow the manufacturing rules, and does it match the netlist?

Background: what are DRC and LVS?

The factory defines dimension rules that must be obeyed when making a chip (minimum wire width, minimum spacing and so on). DRC (Design Rule Check) checks, at the level of shapes, that the finished layout does not break these rules.

LVS (Layout Versus Schematic) extracts the transistors and connections from the layout shapes and compares them with the schematic (the netlist) to see whether they are the same circuit. It can find wrong connections and the like.

Even with correct timing, a chip that breaks a manufacturing rule or has a wrong connection cannot be made. So physical verification is also part of signoff.

The tool is Siemens Calibre. Even if timing is met, whether it can be manufactured and whether it is the circuit of the netlist are separate questions.

DRC (Design Rule Check)

It checks the GDS against manufacturing rules such as minimum wire width, spacing and density.

What you seeMeaning
Violations per ruleRULECHECK ... Result Count in *.drc.summary. Read the ones that are not 0.
Rules meant for the whole chip (seal ring, boundary)Always appear for a stand-alone block. They are resolved at full-chip level, so they may be excluded at block level.
Dummy-pattern / density rulesResolved in the fill phase. They often remain on a stand-alone block.

In this course there are no real geometric rule violations in the wiring; what remains are density, dummy and whole-chip rules (the list is in verif/drc/drc.nonzero.txt).

LVS (Layout Versus Schematic)

It extracts the transistors and connections from the layout and compares them with the netlist (the schematic side).

  1. Convert the netlist to a transistor-level SPICE (the contents of the cells come from the library's SPICE).
  2. Extract the layout and compare device counts, net counts and connections.
  3. Read CORRECT / INCORRECT at the top of the report, then INITIAL NUMBERS OF OBJECTS (layout vs source counts).

How to read the report

WhereMeaning
OVERALL COMPARISON RESULTSCORRECT means pass
INITIAL NUMBERS OF OBJECTSDo the MOS transistor counts match? (used to separate "devices match but connections differ")
INCORRECT NETS / INCORRECT INSTANCESWhich nets and devices disagree

A real example: LVS failed because of power

While building this course, LVS became INCORRECT. This is how it was investigated.

  1. INITIAL NUMBERS OF OBJECTS showed that the transistor counts matched. The parts are right, so it is a connection problem.
  2. LVS on a single standard cell gave CORRECT. The inside of the cell is fine.
  3. Reading INCORRECT INSTANCES: the bulk side of the transistors (b:) was VDD/VSS in the schematic but an unnamed separate net in the layout.
  4. Looking at the power wiring data: some standard-cell power rails were not connected to the upper power straps by vias (the vias had been deleted because they violated design rules).
  5. Using vertical straps only and choosing the via shape again, then re-running: LVS CORRECT, and DRC shows no rule violation.
What you learn
Even with a broken power connection, timing analysis (which assumes ideal power) and DRC pass. Only LVS really checks that the wires are connected.
Block alone versus full chip
A stand-alone block without power pins, seal ring or I/O produces its own violations and mismatches in both DRC and LVS. Telling apart what is just a result of being a block and what is a real problem is the skill of physical verification.

Exercises

  1. Classify the rules in drc.nonzero.txt into "wiring geometry", "density / dummy" and "whole chip".
  2. Temporarily break the via specification in icc2/icc2.tcl (for example change the strap layer), run again, and read INCORRECT NETS to see how LVS fails.
