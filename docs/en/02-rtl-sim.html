02 RTL and Simulation

The example MAC4 and a self-checking testbench.

Background: what are RTL and simulation?

RTL (Register Transfer Level) is a level of abstraction where a circuit is written as "registers (parts that remember a value) and the calculations between them". You write things like "at every clock, add these two values and store the result in this register" in a language called Verilog.

A software program runs from top to bottom, but a hardware description says "all the parts are working at the same time". This is the first thing that confuses newcomers.

The clock is a signal ticking at a fixed interval. A register takes in a new value only at the rising edge of the clock. A digital circuit advances one step at a time to this beat.

Simulation reproduces the behaviour of the circuit on a computer. A testbench is a "circuit for testing" that feeds inputs to your circuit and checks the outputs. It does not go into the real chip.

  • After editing the RTL just run make sim again (it recompiles every time).
  • What the script does: it gives the RTL and the testbench to xrun (the simulator). -access +r makes signals readable and -define DUMP writes the waveform.

Example: four-lane multiply-accumulate (MAC4)

One lane multiplies two 4-bit numbers and adds the product to a 12-bit accumulator; its inputs are registered. Four lanes are placed side by side and share the clock and reset (about 90 flip-flops). It is small, yet large enough to need a clock tree.

The left side is the top level with four lanes; the right side is the inside of one lane. Data flows input registers → multiply → add → accumulator, and the accumulator value is fed back into the next cycle's addition.

Testbench

Random inputs go to the DUT and to a behavioural model at the same time, and the outputs are compared every cycle. A PASS/FAIL result without looking at waveforms is important. The same testbench is reused for the gate-level simulation later.

Pitfall: when reset is released
If reset is released exactly on a clock edge, the gate-level model reports a recovery violation and the outputs become X (RTL does not show this). The testbench releases reset at @(negedge clk).
